The question

I play Death Mark from my trash for its Flow cost. My opponent counters it with Defy. Death Mark's Flow cost is [1][A][A], which is more Power than Defy allows — can Defy even target it? And if it resolves, does Death Mark go to my trash (so I could Flow it again) or is it banished?

As the rules see ittwo questions: does Defy's cost restriction read Death Mark's base cost or the Flow cost actually paid, and does Counter's "placed in the trash" or Flow's delayed banish win when a Flow spell is cleared from the chain?

YES 7/7 citations verified verbatim

Yes, Defy can counter it1 — cost restrictions read the base cost, not the Flow cost you paid2 — and Death Mark is then banished rather than trashed⌁4, so you cannot Flow it a second time⌁5.

Reasoning

1

Defy's restriction is measured against Death Mark's printed cost of 2 Energy and 1 Power, which is within both limits.

● grounded

Defy counters "a spell that costs no more than [4] and no more than [A]". Death Mark is printed at 2 Energy / 1 Power. Its Flow cost of [1][A][A] is louder but irrelevant — see n2.

4

Flow's banish is a delayed replacement effect, so it replaces that trip to the trash.

▲ structuralCrux

Death Mark became a finalized chain item when it was played, and Counter — not Death Mark's own execution — is what removes it. Both conditions in 829.1.b.1 are met, so "banish it instead" replaces 425.1.a.1's trash placement. No single rule states the outcome for a countered Flow spell; it follows from the replacement applying to this exact departure.

If this is wrongIf the banish only applied to a spell that resolves, a countered Death Mark would sit in the trash and could be Flowed again on a later turn. The first half of the answer is unaffected either way.

The argument against, and why it loses

The opposing reading

“The banish is part of "Then banish it" in Flow's own wording, so it only happens after the spell successfully resolves. A countered spell never resolves, so it just goes to the trash like any other countered card.”

Why it loses

829.1.b.1 exists precisely to say that this is not sequencing but a delayed replacement, and it is written around the spell leaving the chain rather than around it resolving. Being countered is a departure from the chain that Death Mark's own execution did not instruct, which is the case the rule describes.
