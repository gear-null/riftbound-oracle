The question

Explain the HOT FEPR loop as best you can

As the rules see itthe process rule 334 names HOT FEPR — how a player works through outstanding Tasks and then through the Chain, and which condition sends play from each step to the next.

HOT FEPR 33/33 citations verified verbatim

Anything that incurs a Task runs the same loop: clear every Task first, then work the Chain one item at a time through Finalize, Execute, Pass and Resolve — where most steps can send you backwards, which is why one Chain runs the loop many times before play moves on.

Step by step

1

H — Handle Outstanding Tasks

● grounded

A Task is anything one or more players must finish before anything else may happen, so nothing else proceeds while one is outstanding. Chain Items created while you work through Tasks are not lost — they sit on the Chain until the Tasks are done. H is also not only the first step: a Task incurred partway through FEPR pauses the process, and you complete the step you are in, handle the Task, then carry on from where you were. That is a pause and resume rather than a restart, which is why no arrow on the map returns here.

CR 333 ✓ verified
  1. 333 A Task is one or more steps or processes that one or more Players must perform before continuing with any other actions.

2

F — Finalize

● grounded

The oldest pending item goes first: its controller completes the steps of playing it until it is Finalized or leaves the Chain, and items finalize in the order they were appended. Finalizing is bookkeeping, not a window to act in — it does not pass Priority. Note the numbering: the rules call Finalize "Step 1", so "return to step 1" inside these rules means Finalize, not Handle Outstanding Tasks.

3

E — Execute

● grounded

The player with Priority chooses: play something legally timed, or pass. Those are the only two options on offer — the rules give the player with Priority this list and nothing else. "Legally timed" is the constraint that matters: by default cards and activated abilities cannot be played in a Closed State, so what qualifies here is generally something with Reaction.

4

P — Pass

● grounded

Pass asks one question: has every player passed in sequence without adding anything to the Chain? That is the test for whether the window has actually closed, and playing anything at all resets it.

5

R — Resolve

● grounded

Exactly one item resolves — the newest Finalized one — and its effects are executed in their entirety. One item, not the whole Chain: what happens next depends entirely on what is still sitting on it, and the three transitions below are the whole answer.

Commonly got wrong

Commonly believed

“You can respond to a unit being played, the way you respond to a spell.”

What the rules actually say

A Unit resolves the moment it is finalized. It moves straight to Resolve without passing through Execute, so the window in which anyone could answer it never opens.

CR 337.2 ✓ verified
  1. 337 Step 1: Finalize
  2. 337.2 If, after finalizing the Chain Item, that item is a Unit, Gear, or an ability that Adds resources, it resolves immediately—Move to Step 4: Resolve. See rule 349. Playing Cards for more information.
Open in rulebook →
Commonly believed

“Once you are in FEPR you run it to the end before anything else happens.”

What the rules actually say

A Task incurred during the process interrupts it. You finish the step you are in, handle the Task, and only then continue — which is the H in HOT FEPR doing work in the middle of the loop, not just at its start.

CR 334.2.a ✓ verified
  1. 334 Whenever a Player takes one or more actions that incur Tasks they should refer to the process of HOT FEPR: Handle Outstanding Tasks; then Finalize, Execute, Pass, Resolve.
  2. 334.2 When all Outstanding Tasks are completed, all pending Chain Items will subsequently be processed by the FEPR process.
  3. 334.2.a During the FEPR process, new Tasks may be incurred. Complete the current step of the process and then pause and complete the necessary Tasks before continuing.

Commonly believed

“When everyone passes, the Chain resolves.”

What the rules actually say

One item resolves. If anything is left on the Chain afterwards you go back into the loop — to Finalize if something is pending, to Execute if not — so a Chain of three resolves in three separate passes with a window of opportunity between each.

Considered and rejected

  • CR:305 — Describes when a phase ends once the Chain is empty — the frame around this loop, not a step inside it.
  • CR:341 — Showdowns are a window FEPR runs inside; the alternation of Focus is a separate process from the Priority passing in step 3.
