Live output

Two documents, exactly as the skill wrote them

Neither of these was touched after it was generated. They are the real files a run produces: one HTML document each, carrying their own CSS and JavaScript, so they can be kept or sent on with nothing attached.

Open one and click a rule ID. The full rulebook opens over the document at that exact clause, so you never lose your place in the argument.

Sample 1 · a ruling

Death Mark,
countered by Defy

I play Death Mark from my trash for its Flow cost. My opponent counters it with Defy. Death Mark's Flow cost is [1][A][A], which is more Power than Defy allows — can Defy even target it? And if it resolves, does Death Mark go to my trash (so I could Flow it again) or is it banished?

Three questions in one, resting on the difference between a card's base cost and the cost you actually paid. The verdict is YES, and it is typed one clause at a time with a superscript on each clause pointing at the numbered claim that carries it.

It is a good one to audit because its weakest link is not its conclusion: claim 4 is structural — no single rule says it, it follows from the ones cited — and because confidence is the floor rather than the average, that one claim sets the confidence of the whole answer. The report says so on the verdict plate rather than making you find it.

Sample 2 · a primer

The HOT FEPR loop,
explained end to end

Explain the HOT FEPR loop as best you can

Asked how something works rather than what happens in one situation, the skill writes a primer instead: no verdict, no crux, five numbered steps, and the twelve transitions between them each carrying the rule that says so.

This is the strictest document the skill produces. A transition's default basis is grounded, so an uncited "this sends you to step 4" fails verification outright rather than rendering as confident prose — which is the one place a reader acts on a sentence without re-reading it.

How to read one

What each part of a report means

The verdict line
One sentence, typed. Each part carries a superscript pointing at the numbered claim that supports it — …played this turn⌁5. A ⌁ before the number means that step is inferred rather than stated outright.
Basis, on every claim
● grounded — a rule states this in so many words. ▲ structural — no single rule says it; it follows from the rules cited. ○ gap — the rules don't address it. Glyph and word both, so the basis is never carried by colour alone.
Weakest link
Confidence is min() across claims, never an average. Nine grounded claims plus one structural makes a structural answer, and a single gap makes a gap answer however solid the rest is.
Crux
Exactly one claim is marked CRUX: the load-bearing one. It must state what happens if it is wrong and whether the holding flips — which collapses auditing an answer from N claims to one.
Citations
Each expands in place to show the cited rule with its ancestor spine, because a rule is meaningless without the clause it sits under. The ✓ VERIFIED stamp means code opened that rule and found the quoted span inside it; a failure inverts to a solid ✗ UNVERIFIED plate rather than merely changing hue, so it survives a glance, a greyscale printer and a reader who does not know the palette.
Counterargument
The opposing reading is stated at full strength, by rule, and then rejected. A reader who has already read the contrary rule will not believe the holding until it is confronted.
Cards referenced
Artwork, structured stats and printed text with Riot's published errata applied. Artwork is loaded from Riot's CDN by URL; no image is stored in this project, and a reader with no network sees a labelled "artwork offline" placeholder.
Printing
The screen document is dark by design. Printing inverts the whole system to a clean sheet: dark ink, Gold 700 hairlines, every citation forced open so no evidence hides, and the index dropped. Judges print these.

The full account is in docs/report-anatomy.md, including the five dispositions and why ANSWER exists alongside YES / NO / DEPENDS / UNSETTLED.

One qualification

Card artwork is the only thing not inside these files. They reference Riot's CDN by URL rather than redistributing the images, so a reader with no network gets a labelled "artwork offline" placeholder and everything else — the argument, the citations, the rulebook links — works normally. Setting RIFTBOUND_EMBED_ART=1 inlines the art at roughly 1MB per card, for viewers that block remote images.
