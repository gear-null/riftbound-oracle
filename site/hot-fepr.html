HOT FEPR

Anything that incurs a Task runs the same loop: clear every Task first, then work the Chain one item at a time through Finalize, Execute, Pass and Resolve — where most steps can send you backwards, which is why one Chain runs the loop many times before play moves on.

This page covers the process rule 334 names HOT FEPR — how a player works through outstanding Tasks and then through the Chain, and which condition sends play from each step to the next.

The map

The steps

1

H — Handle Outstanding Tasks

● Grounded

A Task is anything one or more players must finish before anything else may happen, so nothing else proceeds while one is outstanding. Chain Items created while you work through Tasks are not lost — they sit on the Chain until the Tasks are done. H is also not only the first step: a Task incurred partway through FEPR pauses the process, and you complete the step you are in, handle the Task, then carry on from where you were. That is a pause and resume rather than a restart, which is why no arrow on the map returns here.

  • CR 333A Task is one or more steps or processes that one or more Players must perform before continuing with any other actions.
  • CR 334Whenever a Player takes one or more actions that incur Tasks they should refer to the process of HOT FEPR: Handle Outstanding Tasks; then Finalize, Execute, Pass, Resolve.
  • CR 334.1In the course of Handling Outstanding Tasks, Chain Items may be added to the Chain. They will remain there until the Tasks are complete.
  • CR 334.2.aDuring the FEPR process, new Tasks may be incurred. Complete the current step of the process and then pause and complete the necessary Tasks before continuing.

Where you go next

  • 1
    every outstanding Task is complete and one or more Chain Items are pending Go to step 2 — F — Finalize

    Because CR 336

  • 2
    every outstanding Task is complete and nothing is pending on the Chain — there is no Chain to work, so the loop does not run The procedure ends here

    Because CR 335

2

F — Finalize

● Grounded

The oldest pending item goes first: its controller completes the steps of playing it until it is Finalized or leaves the Chain, and items finalize in the order they were appended. Finalizing is bookkeeping, not a window to act in — it does not pass Priority. Note the numbering: the rules call Finalize "Step 1", so "return to step 1" inside these rules means Finalize, not Handle Outstanding Tasks.

  • CR 337Step 1: Finalize
  • CR 337.1If there is at least one Chain Item Pending, the controller of the oldest Pending Chain Item must complete the steps of Playing that Pending Item until it is a Finalized Item or leaves the Chain.
  • CR 337.1.aFinalizing an item to the chain does not pass Priority.
  • CR 337.1.bChain Items are Finalized in the order they were appended to the Chain.

Where you go next

  • 3
    the item just finalized is a Unit, Gear, or an ability that Adds resources — it never waits on the Chain to be answered Go to step 5 — R — Resolve

    Because CR 337.2

  • 4
    there are still Pending Chain Items after finalizing that one Go to step 2 — F — Finalize

    Because CR 337.3

  • 5
    nothing is left on the Chain to finalize Go to step 3 — E — Execute

    Because CR 337.4

3

E — Execute

● Grounded

The player with Priority chooses: play something legally timed, or pass. Those are the only two options on offer — the rules give the player with Priority this list and nothing else. "Legally timed" is the constraint that matters: by default cards and activated abilities cannot be played in a Closed State, so what qualifies here is generally something with Reaction.

  • CR 338.1The player with Priority may do any the following:
  • CR 338.1.aPlay a Card or Activated Ability that is legally timed.
  • CR 338.1.a.1Cards and Activated Abilities, by default, cannot be played during a Closed State.
  • CR 338.1.a.2A Legally Timed Card or Activated Ability would be one with Reaction or one that will have Reaction when played under appropriate circumstances.
  • CR 338.1.bPass Priority

Where you go next

  • 6
    the player with Priority plays a card or activates an ability, which puts new Pending Items on the Chain Go to step 2 — F — Finalize

    Because CR 338.1.a.7

  • 7
    the player with Priority passes instead Go to step 4 — P — Pass

    Because CR 338.1.b.1

4

P — Pass

● Grounded

Pass asks one question: has every player passed in sequence without adding anything to the Chain? That is the test for whether the window has actually closed, and playing anything at all resets it.

  • CR 339.1If all players have passed Priority in sequence without adding any items to the Chain, proceed to Step 4: Resolve.
  • CR 339.2Otherwise, the player with Priority passes Priority to the next Player in Turn Order. Return to Step 2: Execute.

Where you go next

  • 8
    every player has passed in sequence with nothing added to the Chain Go to step 5 — R — Resolve

    Because CR 339.1

  • 9
    the sequence of passes is not complete, so Priority moves on to the next player Go to step 3 — E — Execute

    Because CR 339.2

5

R — Resolve

● Grounded

Exactly one item resolves — the newest Finalized one — and its effects are executed in their entirety. One item, not the whole Chain: what happens next depends entirely on what is still sitting on it, and the three transitions below are the whole answer.

  • CR 340.1The newest Finalized Chain Item resolves. Execute its game effects in their entirety.

Where you go next

  • 10
    the Chain is now empty The procedure ends here

    Because CR 340.2

  • 11
    the Chain is not empty and one or more items on it are still pending Go to step 2 — F — Finalize

    Because CR 340.3

  • 12
    the Chain is not empty but nothing on it is pending Go to step 3 — E — Execute

    Because CR 340.4

Commonly believed, and wrong

  • You can respond to a unit being played, the way you respond to a spell.

    Why that's wrongA Unit resolves the moment it is finalized. It moves straight to Resolve without passing through Execute, so the window in which anyone could answer it never opens.

    CR 337.2

  • Once you are in FEPR you run it to the end before anything else happens.

    Why that's wrongA Task incurred during the process interrupts it. You finish the step you are in, handle the Task, and only then continue — which is the H in HOT FEPR doing work in the middle of the loop, not just at its start.

    CR 334.2.a

  • When everyone passes, the Chain resolves.

    Why that's wrongOne item resolves. If anything is left on the Chain afterwards you go back into the loop — to Finalize if something is pending, to Execute if not — so a Chain of three resolves in three separate passes with a window of opportunity between each.

    CR 340.1 · CR 340.3 · CR 340.4
